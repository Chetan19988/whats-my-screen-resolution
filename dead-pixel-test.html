What Are Dead Pixels

Understanding pixel defects on flat-panel displays.

Bright pixel (stuck pixel)

A pixel permanently stuck in an illuminated state, appearing as a constantly glowing dot on dark backgrounds. Usually a sub-pixel stuck in the on state. May sometimes be recovered using pixel repair tools.

Dead pixel

A completely failed pixel that remains black regardless of content. Most easily detected on light or white backgrounds. Typically hardware-level damage that cannot be repaired via software.

Stuck pixel

A pixel fixed at one color (red, green, blue or combination) that does not change with the image. Unlike dead pixels, stuck pixels still respond and have a chance of recovery through color cycling.

How to Test for Dead Pixels

Three steps to systematically check for every type of defective pixel.

STEP 01

Prepare environment

Set brightness to maximum and turn off night mode, blue light filter and any display enhancement. Dim lighting yields best results for bright pixel detection.

STEP 02

Fullscreen color cycle

Click a color to enter fullscreen. Use arrow keys to cycle through all 12 solid colors. Carefully scan the entire screen at each color.

STEP 03

Focus on key areas

Dead pixels are most common at screen edges and corners. Scan top to bottom, left to right. Stay on each color for at least 5 seconds.

What Causes Dead Pixels

Manufacturing defects

During panel production, TFT transistors or OLED emissive layers may sustain microscopic damage, causing individual pixels to be defective from the factory.

Physical pressure

Compression during shipping, aggressive cleaning or contact with sharp objects can damage liquid crystal alignment or OLED organic materials.

Electrostatic damage

Electrostatic discharge in dry environments can destroy drive transistors, causing permanent pixel failure.

Natural aging

Over extended use, some pixels drive circuits or emissive materials degrade, especially in areas that frequently display high-brightness content.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q.Can dead pixels be repaired?

Bright pixels and stuck pixels may recover through rapid color flashing programs. Dead pixels that stay black generally cannot be fixed via software. Try a repair tool for 30-60 minutes before seeking replacement.

Q.Can I return a new screen with dead pixels?

Depends on brand policy. Apple and Dell Premium Panel have zero bright-pixel tolerance. Most brands follow ISO 13406-2 Class II. Test immediately upon delivery and file any claim within the return window.

Q.Why are 12 different colors needed?

A pixel has red, green and blue sub-pixels. Using only black and white detects completely dead pixels. RGB primaries plus complementary colors detect individual stuck sub-pixels. Grey tones reveal brightness differences.

Q.Does this work on phone screens?

Yes. The tool supports fullscreen mode on phones, tablets, laptops, monitors and TVs. Mobile users can tap or swipe left and right to change colors.

Q.Dead pixels vs OLED burn-in?

Dead pixels are single pixel-level defects. Burn-in is uneven aging across large areas, appearing as ghost images. Both can be detected with solid colors but causes and remedies differ completely.

Q.What to do after finding dead pixels?

Photograph and document locations. Check your brand return policy. For bright or stuck pixels try a repair tool first. If within warranty and exceeding allowed limits, contact the manufacturer.

Ghosting Testing Tips

Get the most reliable result from this visual test.

  • Max brightness: Set screen brightness to 100% before testing. Some defective pixels are hard to spot at low brightness levels.
  • Disable filters: Make sure night mode, blue light filter, HDR enhancement and other post-processing are turned off to avoid interfering with test results.
  • Dark room: Testing in a dimly lit environment yields the best results, especially when checking for bright pixels on pure black backgrounds.
  • Scan patiently: Stay on each color for at least 5 seconds. Slowly scan the entire panel from top to bottom, left to right, edge to edge.
Important: This page is a visual inspection aid. Browsers cannot read panel hardware or pixel health. Use the solid color patterns to compare settings, then rely on manufacturer warranty policy or calibrated equipment for formal decisions.
