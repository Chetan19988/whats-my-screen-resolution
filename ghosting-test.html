Evaluate motion performance with moving-object tracking, overdrive artifact checks and response-time context.

Motion ghosting detection

Color blocks slide across the screen so you can watch for a translucent trail. Longer trails suggest slower response. VA panels usually show the worst dark-tone smear; OLED is virtually ghost-free.

Overdrive artifact detection

Overdrive accelerates pixel response, but over-acceleration creates bright halos ahead of moving objects. Use the test to find the optimal overdrive setting on your monitor.

Multi-speed comparison

Slow suits office scrolling, medium fits video playback and fast approximates FPS gaming. Judge ghosting at the speed you use most for the most meaningful result.

What Is Ghosting?

How pixel response time affects motion image quality.

Pixel response time

Time a pixel needs to change from one color to another, measured in milliseconds. Advertised 1ms is usually the fastest grey-to-grey transition. Real response varies by transition — dark-to-bright can be 3–5× slower than bright-to-dark.

Ghosting vs motion blur

Ghosting is a shadow caused by slow pixel response — an object drags a semi-transparent trail. Motion blur is perceptual blur from eyes tracking sample-and-hold displays and needs black frame insertion or strobed backlighting to reduce.

Overdrive & inverse ghosting

Displays apply higher voltage to speed up pixel response. Moderate overdrive reduces ghosting; excessive overdrive makes pixels overshoot, creating bright reverse artifacts that can be more distracting than no overdrive at all.

How to Test for Ghosting

Three steps to evaluate your display's motion performance.

STEP 01

Watch moving blocks

Focus behind the moving blocks: is there a translucent trail? Trail visibility reflects response time. Black backgrounds reveal the most issues, especially VA dark-tone smear.

STEP 02

Adjust overdrive

In your monitor OSD, find Response Time or Overdrive and cycle Off/Normal/Fast/Fastest. At each level check: are trails reduced? Do white halos appear in front?

STEP 03

Multi-speed verification

Increase from slow to fast. Office users mainly need clean motion at lower speeds; FPS gamers need clean motion even at high speeds.

Ghosting Terminology

Key terms you will see in monitor specifications and reviews.

GtG response time

Grey-to-grey — time for a pixel to switch between grey levels. The advertised value is typically the fastest single transition; real average GtG is often 2–4× the spec.

MPRT

Moving Picture Response Time combines pixel response with sample-and-hold persistence blur. It better matches perceived motion clarity. At 60Hz the minimum MPRT is about 16.67ms.

Overdrive / OD

Pixel acceleration that briefly applies higher voltage to speed liquid-crystal rotation. Built into virtually all LCDs, usually with Off/Low/Medium/High settings. Start with Medium.

VRR & ghosting

FreeSync/G-Sync solve screen tearing, not ghosting. At low frame rates, VRR can worsen overdrive behaviour on some displays and increase overshoot artifacts.

Ghosting Performance by Panel Type

How panel technology affects motion quality.

TN panel

1–4ms GtG
  • Fastest LCD type for average response.
  • Dark transitions are also relatively fast.
  • Minimal ghosting, easy to tune overdrive.
  • Historical first choice for competitive esports.

IPS panel

3–8ms GtG
  • Fast IPS reaches 1–3ms average response.
  • Dark transitions slightly slower than TN.
  • Modern Fast IPS ghosting approaches TN.
  • Overdrive Medium is usually recommended.

VA panel

5–40ms dark GtG
  • Bright transitions: 3–5ms, acceptable.
  • Dark transitions: 15–40ms, severe ghosting.
  • Most visible in dark scenes (“VA smear”).
  • Overdrive has limited dark-tone improvement.

OLED

<0.1ms GtG
  • All color transitions are extremely fast.
  • Virtually no ghosting in the traditional sense.
  • No overdrive setting is needed.
  • Best motion performance of any panel type.

Ghosting Optimization Tips

Practical settings to reduce trails and inverse artifacts.

Adjust overdrive

Start from the middle setting. White halos mean go down one level; heavy trails mean go up one level. Find the balance. OLED does not need this setting.

Increase refresh rate

Higher refresh shortens inter-frame hold time and lowers perceived blur. Going from 60Hz to 144Hz greatly improves dynamic clarity — but slow pixels can expose more ghosting at higher rates.

Black frame insertion

BFI inserts black frames between real frames to reduce sample-and-hold blur. It trades some brightness for significantly better motion clarity. Some displays brand this as ELMB or DyAc.

Gaming optimization

VA dark-scene ghosting hits FPS games hardest. If you cannot switch panels, raise in-game brightness or gamma to reduce dark scenes, and disable in-game motion blur effects.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q.Why does my “1ms” display still show ghosting?

The advertised 1ms is the single fastest transition among all colors. Other transitions may be 5–20ms, and VA panels advertising 1ms can still have dark transitions of 30ms+.

Q.Can VA panel dark ghosting be fixed?

Not fundamentally. VA dark transitions are inherently slow. Overdrive helps only a little, and pushing it too high creates inverse artifacts. Premium VAs have improved but still trail IPS/OLED.

Q.Should I max out overdrive?

Generally no. Maximum overdrive almost always creates severe overshoot — bright leading halos on moving objects. The middle setting is usually the best balance.

Q.Is higher refresh rate always less ghosting?

Not entirely. Higher refresh reduces hold-time blur, but if pixel response is too slow, more ghosting can be exposed because more color switches must finish in the same time.

Q.Does OLED truly have zero ghosting?

OLED response is around 0.03–0.1ms, so no traditional ghosting — but OLED still has sample-and-hold blur, which needs high refresh or BFI to reduce.

Q.Is black frame insertion worth using?

Very effective — it can cut MPRT from about 16ms to a few ms — but brightness drops 40–60% and low-frequency BFI can show perceptible flicker.

Ghosting Testing Tips

Get the most reliable result from this visual test.

  • Background matters: light objects on a black background expose ghosting the most. Also test dark objects on a white background for the reverse case.
  • Disable VRR: turn off FreeSync/G-Sync first — some displays change overdrive behaviour in VRR mode.
  • Test step by step: don't jump straight to max overdrive. Start from Off and increase one level at a time, watching trails and inverse artifacts carefully.
  • Real-world verification: after tool testing, confirm in your most-used games or videos. Real experience matters more than a single test pattern.
