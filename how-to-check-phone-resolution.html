How to Check Phone Resolution

Need to find your exact screen dimensions? Learn how to check phone resolution instantly on Android, iPhone, or using a free online tool.

Whether you are testing an app, comparing displays, or optimizing wallpapers, knowing how to check my phone resolution is straightforward. This guide skips the fluff and gives you the exact steps for Android, iOS, and browser-based methods.

TL;DR — The Quick Summary

  • The Universal Way: Use our online resolution checker for an instant reading on any phone without opening settings.
  • Android Phones: Go to Settings > Display > Screen resolution.
  • iPhones: Apple hides this in iOS. Check Apple's tech specs online for your exact model's physical pixel count.

1. The Instant Phone Resolution Check (Web Tool)

The absolute fastest way to check phone resolution without digging through system menus is by using a free browser-based detector. This works universally across all brands and operating systems.

Steps:

  1. Open Chrome, Safari, or your preferred mobile browser.

2. How to Check Screen Resolution Android Devices

Many Android manufacturers (like Samsung, OnePlus, and Google) allow you to view and manually adjust your display resolution directly within the operating system to save battery or boost visual quality.

Steps:

  1. Open the Settings app on your Android device.
  2. Scroll down and tap on Display.
  3. Look for the Screen resolution option. (Note: On some devices, you may need to tap "Advanced" first).
  4. You will see your current active resolution (e.g., HD+, FHD+ 2400 × 1080, or WQHD+).

3. Finding Resolution on an iPhone

Apple does not natively display physical pixel counts inside the iOS settings menu. If you need to run a phone resolution check on an iPhone or iPad, you have two options:

Steps:

  • Use an online tool: Open Safari and use our web-based resolution checker. Keep in mind that iPhones use Device Pixel Ratio (DPR) scaling, so the tool will show your CSS viewport (e.g., 393 × 852), which maps to a higher physical pixel count.
  • Check Apple's Tech Specs: Go to Apple.com, find your specific iPhone model, and view the tech specs. Under the "Display" section, it will list the exact physical resolution (e.g., the iPhone 15 Pro is exactly 2556 × 1179 pixels).

4. Check the Manufacturer Specifications

If your phone won't turn on, the screen is broken, or you simply want the hardware spec sheet rather than software settings, looking up the device model is the best approach.

Steps:

  1. Identify your exact phone model (usually printed on the back of the device, on the original box, or under Settings > About Phone).
  2. Do a web search for "[Your Phone Model] display specifications".
  3. Look for the "Display" or "Screen" section on the manufacturer's official site or a trusted database like GSMArena to find the exact pixel height and width.
