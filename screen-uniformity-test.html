What a Screen Uniformity Test Checks

Fullscreen patterns help your eyes compare different areas of the panel.

◐

Brightness uniformity

Gray and white fields make visible changes in brightness from the center to the edges easier to compare.

◒

Gray uniformity

Mid-gray and dark-gray patterns can reveal tint variation, clouding, shading or dirty-screen effects.

◉

Color consistency

Red, green, blue and neutral patterns help you notice visible color patches or uneven tint across the display.

1

How to Test Screen Uniformity

Follow a consistent visual inspection routine.

STEP 01

Prepare a neutral setup

Use a comfortable brightness, disable automatic picture changes if practical, and keep room lighting consistent while you compare patterns.

STEP 02

Inspect fullscreen gray fields

Start with 50% gray, then compare darker and lighter gray. Look across the center, sides, edges and corners for visible changes.

STEP 03

Compare other patterns

Check white and black, then red, green and blue. Repeat any pattern where you noticed an unusual tint, patch or brightness shift.

i

Understanding What You See

This page displays patterns for a human visual comparison; it does not generate a pass/fail score.

Uneven brightness or tint

If part of a uniform field looks visibly lighter, darker or differently tinted, compare the same region on other patterns and at your normal viewing position. Ambient light, viewing angle, display mode and local dimming can affect perception.

Dark-scene clouding

Dark gray can make some panel shading or clouding easier to notice. Check whether it is visible in typical use before deciding whether it matters to you.

Visual reference, not a measurement

A browser cannot measure panel luminance or color difference with this pattern. For quantitative uniformity results, use a suitable calibrated meter and controlled conditions. This tool does not diagnose or certify hardware.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a screen uniformity test?

It displays fullscreen gray, white, black and color patterns so you can visually inspect for uneven brightness, shading, tint or color patches.

How do I check brightness uniformity?

Use consistent room lighting, disable automatic display changes if practical, and compare fullscreen gray and white patterns across the center, edges and corners.

Does this tool measure or certify uniformity?

No. It provides visual reference patterns only. Quantitative luminance or color-difference testing requires suitable calibrated instruments.

Why use gray patterns?

Mid-gray and dark-gray fields can make subtle shading, tint variation, clouding or dirty-screen effects easier to notice.

Can I use this on a phone, laptop or TV?

Yes. Open the page in a modern browser, use fullscreen where available and inspect at a normal viewing distance.
